01 WHO I AM

Engineering was never just one discipline for me.

My background started with Mechatronics Engineering, where mechanical systems, electronics, control, programming and automation came together.

But the more systems I built, the more I wanted to understand everything around them — from the physical machine and its sensors to the software, data and intelligence controlling it.

That curiosity became the path behind everything I have built since.

THE JOURNEY

From building machines to building intelligent systems.

A story of moving from physical systems, to robotics and automation, to software, data and intelligent technology.

01
FOUNDATION

It started with Mechatronics.

At Ain Shams University, I studied Mechatronics Engineering — a field that naturally connected mechanical engineering, electrical systems, electronics, control and programming.

Instead of seeing these disciplines separately, I learned to think about them as parts of one complete system.

02
COMPETITION

Then I learned what engineering feels like under pressure.

I worked with a team on a Water Turbine project sponsored by the Center of Excellence of Energy and Arizona State University.

We developed an undershot water turbine and achieved 60% efficiency — ultimately reaching 1st place among 42 teams.

It was more than a competition. It taught me how to turn theory into a working design, iterate, solve problems and make engineering decisions as a team.

03
ROBOTICS & AUTOMATION

I wanted machines to become autonomous.

That led me deeper into robotics and industrial automation.

I designed a fully automated production line with Feeding, Sorting, Assembly and Packaging stations, integrating two robotic arms and machine vision.

I worked with SolidWorks for system design, robotic mechanisms, PID control and MATLAB for actuator sizing and selection.

Robotics Machine Vision PID SolidWorks MATLAB
04
GRADUATION PROJECT

From automation to autonomy.

For my graduation project, I built an Autonomous Shelf Lift Robot.

The system combined ROS1, LiDAR, EKF-based sensor fusion, autonomous navigation, localization, a custom lifting mechanism and a charging station with BMS monitoring.

I validated the system in both Gazebo simulation and real-world testing.

Sensors → Perception → Fusion → Localization → Navigation
05
AI & DATA

Then I started asking different questions.

What if systems could not only execute commands, but learn from information?

I expanded into Python, SQL, data analysis, machine learning, deep learning, TensorFlow, OpenCV and Scikit-learn.

During my AI/ML internship at Elevvo Pathways, I worked on practical machine-learning problems, including loan approval, customer clustering and recommendation systems.

06
SOFTWARE

I moved closer to the software behind the system.

Engineering systems depend on software, and that made software quality another important part of my journey.

Through DEPI's Software Testing track, I am developing practical experience with test analysis and design, static testing, Jira, Postman, Selenium, Java and ISTQB-based testing practices.

My engineering background influences how I test: understand the system, identify failure points, investigate behavior and verify the result.

07
REAL-WORLD ENGINEERING

Engineering became more than projects.

At Delotrans, I worked in logistics operations, compliance and coordination, supporting a fleet of 255 trucks and GTG/PTI processing for more than 8,800 drivers.

Today, at Orascom Services, I work as a Technical Office Engineer in Spare Parts Operations, researching Volvo Penta parts catalogs, reviewing engine components and service requirements, and supporting technical spare-parts operations.

These experiences taught me that engineering is not only about making something work. It is also about accuracy, communication, documentation, responsibility and solving real operational problems.

08
TODAY

And the journey is still moving.

Today, my interests sit at the intersection of engineering and technology: robotics, automation, embedded systems, AI, data and software quality.

I am not trying to fit everything I know into one box. I am building the ability to understand complete systems — how they are designed, controlled, programmed, tested and improved.
